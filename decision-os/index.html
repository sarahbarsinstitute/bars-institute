A worked example

Confidence is not the same as evidence

A software business doing $248k a month is considering an 8% price rise. In the model, raising prices comes out ahead. But look at what that result is built on.

Raising prices is worth $231,759 over two years, unless you lose more than 9.5% of customers. Nobody has measured that.

That single sentence is the output. Not the 93%, not the expected value. It is the fact that the entire comparison is sitting on one number the business has never tested, and that the line is only four percentage points away from where they guessed.

It shows what is worth measuring before committing: here, the actual churn on a limited increase in one segment. The analysis identifies the figure to go and find out, and how much precision it needs.

This is what BARS produces that a spreadsheet does not. A spreadsheet gives you a number. This gives you the number, its fragility, and the figure worth measuring first.

Why trust the number

The model calculates. AI does not decide the answer.

Where AI is involved, its job ends at describing the decision as a structured model. A separate calculation engine takes that model and nothing else, so it cannot be talked into an answer. Every default it applies is reported, and where a missing input would decide the result it refuses to run.
