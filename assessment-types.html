Assessment Types

Four engagement types covering vineyard biological risk — from annual health checks through to pre-purchase due diligence for high-value transactions.

Every engagement begins with a signed proposal confirming scope, fees, and visit timing. The comparison below sets out what each engagement type includes — if the right fit isn't immediately clear, contact BARS Institute directly.

All engagement types include a stratified GLRaV-3 survey with GPS waypoints and a Tier 1 trunk disease visual assessment. What differs is the number of field visits, the additional testing applied, and the financial outputs produced.

Add-ons

Optional add-ons

The following are available as separately scoped add-ons across all engagement types. Virus add-ons have specific tissue collection and seasonal timing requirements — contact BARS Institute before engagement to confirm scope.

Spatial map of positive bays

A georeferenced map of all sampled bays, with positive bays marked by location, overlaid on the block boundary polygon and stratification zones. Produced from GPS coordinates collected at assessment — no additional field work required.

Available across all engagement types. For Annual Monitor engagements, the current-year map can be overlaid against the prior-year map to show year-on-year change in infection distribution — useful for distinguishing new boundary ingress from established within-block spread.

GRBV — Grapevine Red Blotch Virus

Available as a biosecurity surveillance add-on for clients seeking to confirm absence in imported material or newly established blocks, or where symptoms consistent with GRBV have been observed. Requires leaf blade tissue collected December–February. Petiole tissue and collection outside this window are not approved for GRBV assessment.

Results are reported as presence/absence only — financial impairment modelling is not applied to GPGV at this time due to insufficient NZ-specific pathogenicity and yield-loss data.

GPGV — Grapevine Pinot Gris Virus

Available for Pinot Gris and Chardonnay blocks where mottling or deformation symptoms are present. Requires a separate spring shoot visit during active shoot growth (September–November). Results are reported as presence/absence only — financial impairment modelling is not applied to GPGV at this time due to insufficient NZ-specific pathogenicity and yield-loss data.

Collection outside the September–November window is not recommended and will be noted as a limitation in the report.
